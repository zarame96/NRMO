\section*{0. TTM Charter (Constitutional Wedge)}
\label{sec:ttm-charter}

\nrmobox{TTM/PPS Charter}{
TTM/PPS is \emph{not} a manual for intentionally manipulating others.
It addresses risk structures such as psychological pressure, unfair
negotiation, and confusion induction \emph{for the purpose of
understanding, detection, and training in defense}. Direct
application to real dialogue, organizational operations, romance,
sales, and similar contexts is \textbf{not performed} (separate
design, separate ethics, and separate agreement are required).\\[0.4em]
\textbf{NRMO alignment.} The purpose is to \emph{preserve future
degrees of freedom}. TTM uses this purpose to learn structures that
\emph{reduce} degrees of freedom.
}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
TTM/PPS is a training layer external to NRMO for understanding and
defending against psychological pressure patterns. \textbf{Transfer
to real-world operation is prohibited.} Direct application to
persuasion, sales, relationships, or organizational management
requires separate design, separate ethics, and separate agreement.

\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\ttfamily\small,frame=single]
START_TTM:
- This is training (no real-world application).

END_TTM:
- Summarise the experience in 3 lines.

- One next defensive move.
- Rest / cool-down.

\section{Safe Stop (SHUTDOWN) Conditions}
\label{sec:ttm-shutdown}

\nrmobox{Immediate stop on any of:}{
\begin{itemize}
\item Bodily symptoms (palpitations, strong anxiety, flashbacks,
etc.).
\item Inability to maintain one's own boundary; sense of being
cornered.
\item Anger, shame, or fear sustained at high intensity.
\item Strong emergence of \textit{``I want to use this in real
life''} (transfer impulse).
\end{itemize}
}

\section{Training Flow (Experience $\to$ Analysis $\to$ Defense)}
\label{sec:ttm-flow}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Experience}: observe the pressure structure (record
what happened).
\item \textbf{Analysis}: identify the category (A--H), and compress
``why it works'' into one sentence.
\item \textbf{Defense}: drop into SOP-conformant A/B actions
(hold, confirmation, splitting, third-party, logging).
\item \textbf{Termination}: release with \texttt{END\_TTM};
cool-down.
\end{enumerate}

\section{TTM Pattern Catalogue (400-Entry Indexing, 200 Unique Rows)}
\label{sec:ttm-catalog}

\nrmobox{How to read this catalogue}{
This is \textbf{not} a procedure for moving others. It is an
\emph{index} for quickly identifying patterns of pressure / distortion
and converting them into reversible defensive actions.
}

\paragraph{Categories.}
A = Pressure / Time;
B = Relationship / Guilt;
C = Logic / Frame;
D = Confusion / Overload;
E = Authority / Role;
F = Agency Erosion;
G = Negotiation Distortion;
H = Emotion Induction.

\paragraph{SOP abbreviation.}
\textit{``A/B; I1$\to$M; V2/L2$\to$S''} = Prioritise A/B-class actions
(hold, confirm, split, third-party). \texttt{IRREV=I1} $\to$
\textbf{MISSION}; \texttt{VOL=V2} or \texttt{LOAD=L2} $\to$
\textbf{SHUTDOWN}.

\paragraph{Indexing scheme.}
The catalogue contains \textbf{40 base patterns}, each instantiated
across \textbf{5 intensity levels} (Mild, Medium, Strong,
High-Strong, Extreme), yielding \textbf{200 catalogue rows}. Each
row corresponds to ``base pattern $\times$ intensity.'' The total of
``$200 \times 2$'' that gives ``400'' refers to including both
Japanese and English views; the 200 unique structural rows are the
authoritative reference.

\paragraph{Detection-sign convention.}
The detection sign is intensity-dependent and shared across all 40
base patterns:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Mild}: signs are weak; the pattern cannot be
asserted yet ().
\item \textbf{Medium} (): the demand or frame is clearly fixed
().
\item \textbf{Strong} (): the right of refusal or retreat begins to
be removed ().
\item \textbf{High-Strong} (): the pressure of isolation or
irreversible commitment is strong ().
\item \textbf{Extreme} (): threats, coercion, or
health/legal risk is involved ().
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Catalogue closing note.}
The 400-entry framing arises from base-pattern $\times$ intensity
expansion (40 base $\times$ 5 intensity $=$ 200 unique rows;
multilingual rendering yields 400). In actual operation, ``connection
to the defensive action'' --- not the pattern \emph{name} --- is the
substance. Pattern names exist to accelerate recognition, not to
attribute personality or motive to the other party.

\textit{A/B}-class action (hold $|$ confirmation $|$ split $|$

\section{Anti-Contamination Requirement}
\label{sec:ttm-sop-integration}

TTM training output must \textbf{not} bypass the Carryback procedure
(Chapter~\ref{ch:secretary-modules}, Section~\ref{sec:carryback-spec}).
Specifically:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Pattern recognition is permitted and produces signals.
\item Pattern \emph{application} is forbidden in any operational
mode.
\item Carryback (Tripwire $\to$ Gate $\to$ PP Template $\to$ SOP
Patch) is the only sanctioned path for findings to reach the
operational layer.
\item Direct ``it worked in training, so use it in operation''
reasoning triggers \textbf{SHUTDOWN}.
\end{enumerate}
