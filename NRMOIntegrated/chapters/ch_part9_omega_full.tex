over-defensive collapse behind earlier ``poor numbers.'' The evaluation
horizon must be long enough to credit compounding growth, otherwise the
choice biases toward over-defense. Maximum forward is the default posture,
not a universal optimum: the civ-sim layer discovers each world's true
optimum (aggression, sustainable growth, or even extreme caution as in
V9-minimum worlds). See the Realignment Master Spec.

\section{Foundational Concepts}
\label{sec:omega-foundational}

\subsection{Absorbing Failure}
\label{sec:omega-absorbing-failure}

An absorbing failure state is a state from which recovery is
impossible, or whose recovery cost is so high that it cannot be
reasonably modelled. Optionality depletion.

\subsection{Optionality}
\label{sec:omega-optionality}

Optionality is the preserved breadth of future action space.
Resources, trust, health, institutional flexibility, temporal slack,
strategic alternatives.

\subsection{Active Ruin and Passive Ruin}
\label{sec:omega-active-vs-passive}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Active ruin}: direct collapse from reckless action or
uncontrolled expansion.
\item \textbf{Passive ruin}: erosion of optionality from long-term
avoidance, rigidity, insufficient exploration, adaptation failure.
\end{itemize}

The former is dramatic; the latter appears stable but often becomes
terminal suddenly. Robust designs must address both.

% =====================================================================
\section{Design Principles}
\label{sec:omega-design-principles}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{True ruin is a hard constraint}: if a candidate action
increases the probability of entering true ruin state above a
threshold, short-circuit.
\item \textbf{Optionality is the long-horizon objective}: within the
non-ruin domain, maintain future manoeuvrability and gain as much
as possible.
\item \textbf{Non-ruin is not enough}: even avoiding dramatic
failure, slow death is possible. Insufficient exploration,
rigidification, decision-space narrowing.
\item \textbf{Intelligence is not authority}: execution capacity,
exploration, and strategy generation must be downstream of
governance constraints.
\end{enumerate}

\section{NRMO vNext Core Reformulation}
\label{sec:omega-core-reformulation}

\textbf{Original}: Avoid absorbing ruin states.

\textbf{Strengthened}:

\begin{equation}
\max\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^H \gamma^t G(S_t, a_t)\right]
\quad \text{subject to} \quad
\Pr(\mathrm{Ruin\ within\ } H) \le \alpha.
\label{eq:omega-strengthened-objective}
\end{equation}

Here $G(S_t, a_t)$ = composite utility including growth, learning,
optionality gain. $\alpha$ = maximum tolerable ruin probability.

\nrmobox{NRMO vNext Core Statement}{
Maximise long-horizon optionality, learning, and durable growth,
subject to avoiding absorbing ruin states and monitoring both active
and passive pathways to collapse.
}

\subsection{Role of Norn}
\label{sec:omega-role-of-norn}

Norn is neither a decision-making subject nor an execution engine.
It is an interpretive governance perspective preserving structural
consistency. It organises context, prevents conceptual drift, and
prevents the constitutional logic from being implicitly bent by
opportunistic short-term reasoning.

\subsection{Why the Split Matters}
\label{sec:omega-split-rationale}

A common failure mode in human and machine systems is the merging
of observation, strategy generation, and authority. Once merged,
the system tends to justify its own impulses. The architecture above
separates search from permission, and anomaly monitoring from
strategy production.

\section{Decision Variables and Exposure Sizing}
\label{sec:omega-decision-vars}

Variables estimated for each candidate action: $R$ (ruin proximity),
$V$ (reversibility), $L$ (learning value), $G$ (expected gain),
$\Delta O$ (optionality effect), $\mathrm{EC}$ (execution cost),
$\mathrm{TC}$ (time cost).

\subsection{Exposure Sizing}
\label{sec:omega-exposure-sizing}

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ExposureAllowed} = E_{\max} \times
\!\left(1 - \frac{R}{100}\right) \times
\frac{V}{100} \times
\!\left(0.5 + 0.5 \cdot \frac{L}{100}\right).
\label{eq:omega-exposure-sizing}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Utility Inside the Non-Ruin Domain}
\label{sec:omega-utility}

\begin{equation}
U = 0.5 G + 0.3 L + 0.2 \Delta O,
\qquad
\mathrm{Score} = U \times \mathrm{ExposureAllowed}.
\label{eq:omega-utility}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Action Generation}
\label{sec:omega-action-generation}

At each time step, $N$ candidate actions are generated. Each action

$\Omega$ Full, the Candidate Population System
(base / mutation / synthesis / invention) generates candidates.

\subsection{Terminal Conditions}
\label{sec:omega-terminal-conditions}

Terminate if any of capital, health, trust, function, optionality
falls below the catastrophic floor.

\subsection{Minimal Pseudocode (Default: Adaptive NRMOvNext +
$\Omega$ Full)}
\label{sec:omega-individual-pseudo}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python,basicstyle=\ttfamily\scriptsize,frame=single]
for episode in episodes:
    state = initialize_agent()
    env   = initialize_environment()
    omega = OmegaFullEngine()
    for t in range(horizon):
        # EXECUTION: candidate generation
        candidates = omega.build_candidate_pool(state, env)

        # GOVERNANCE: admissibility filter
        scored = []
        for action in candidates:
            risk_layer = classify(action.R)
            if risk_layer == R4:
                continue   # hard stop
            exposure = exposure_allowed(action.R,
                                        action.V,
                                        action.L)
            scored.append((action, exposure))
        admissible = nrmo_filter(scored, state)

        # EXECUTION: Omega scoring + portfolio
        chosen = omega.select_action(admissible, state, env)

        state = apply(chosen, state, env)
        if true_ruin(state):
            break
\end{lstlisting}

\section{StrongEngine $\Omega$ Full --- Execution Layer
Specification}
\label{sec:omega-full-spec}

\nrmobox{Definition (source: monograph)}{
StrongEngine $\Omega$ Full = a bounded, failure-aware,
fragility-sensitive, portfolio-capable strategy-space-expansion
engine operating strictly within NRMO admissibility constraints.\\[0.3em]
The engine does \textbf{not} include RUIN in its evaluation function.
RUIN is handled only at the NRMO execution boundary.
}

\subsection{Candidate Population System ($\S 1$)}
\label{sec:omega-candidate-pop}

Candidates are generated from 4 sources:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{base strategies} (11 templates).
\item \textbf{mutated strategies} ($\pm 0.05$ perturbation; 1--3
variants per base).
\item \textbf{synthesised strategies} (pairwise averaging $+
\pm 0.03$ noise).
\item \textbf{invented strategies}
(state $\times$ world analytical candidates).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Pipeline.}
\texttt{base} $\to$ \texttt{mutation} $\to$ \texttt{synthesis} $\to$
\texttt{invention} $\to$ \texttt{candidate\_pool} $\to$
\texttt{governance filter} $\to$ \texttt{evaluation}.

\paragraph{Population size.}
Typically 16--32; 24+ during Wolf Pursuit.

\subsection{Candidate Mutation ($\S 2$)}
\label{sec:omega-mutation}

Local expansion of search space. Add $\mathrm{random}(-0.05, +0.05)$
to each action component. Values clipped to $[0, 1]$ then
re-normalised.

\subsection{Strategy Synthesis ($\S 3$)}
\label{sec:omega-synthesis}

Averaging of two strategies $+$ $\pm 0.03$ noise. 5--10 hybrids
generated from random candidate pairs.

\subsection{Wolf Pursuit Mode ($\S 4$--$\S 5$)}
\label{sec:omega-wolf-pursuit}

Favourable state: $E \ge 50$, $G \ge 45$, $O \ge 45$, $K \ge 45$,
$X \le 35$, $\mathrm{d}O \ge 0$, $\mathrm{d}K \ge 0$. When this
holds, $\texttt{WolfPursuit} = \mathrm{true}$: candidate count
increases, rollout depth increases (depth $= 8$), portfolio
aggression increases.

\subsection{Edge Survival Guard ($\S 6$--$\S 7$)}
\label{sec:omega-edge-guard}

Fragile-condition detection $\to$ $\texttt{EdgeFloor} = \mathrm{true}$:
growth reduction, eco stabilisation, governance repair, low
exposure. Portfolio: main $= 0.45$, hedge $= 0.45$, probe $= 0.10$.

\paragraph{RiskAdjusted Floor.}
In fragile state, if $\Omega$ score $<$ RiskAdj score, switch to
EdgeFloor profile.

\subsection{Portfolio Synergy ($\S 8$)}
\label{sec:omega-portfolio-synergy}

Synergy bonus via pairwise compatibility matrix. In addition to
independent evaluation, candidates are evaluated for candidate
synergy.

\subsection{Dual Objective Scoring ($\S 9$)}
\label{sec:omega-dual-scoring}

Favourable states: maximise $\Omega - \mathrm{RiskAdj}$.
Fragile states: $\Omega \ge \mathrm{RiskAdj}$ as a constraint.

\subsection{Long-Horizon Drift Control}
\label{sec:omega-drift-control}

\begin{equation}
g_{\mathrm{sust}} = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(0.15, 0.40,\;
0.28 + 0.08 \frac{E}{100} + 0.06 \frac{G}{100}
- 0.10 \frac{X}{100} - 0.05 \cdot d_{\mathrm{env}}\right).
\label{eq:omega-gsust}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{drift} = \max(0,\, g - g_{\mathrm{sust}}) \cdot
(d_{\mathrm{env}} + 0.5 p_{\mathrm{tail}}) \cdot
\!\left(1 + 0.5 \frac{X}{100}\right) \cdot
\!\left(1 - 0.3 \frac{G}{100}\right).
\label{eq:omega-drift}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{final\_score} = \mathrm{rollout\_score} -
\lambda_{\mathrm{drift}} \times \mathrm{drift}.
\label{eq:omega-final-score}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Recommended values.}
$\lambda_{\mathrm{drift}} = 1.0$. Normal world: drift $\times 1.25$.

\paragraph{Drift-safe hedge.}
Portfolio hedge candidates satisfying $g \le g_{\mathrm{sust}}$ are
prioritised.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:omega-conclusion}

NRMO is most powerful when understood not as a doctrine of fear but
as a long-horizon navigation system.

The original core is sound: irreversible collapse should dominate
ordinary expected-value calculation. The upgraded system classifies
ruin more carefully, guards passive ruin, allows graded exposure,
and enables constitutional self-repair.

The default stack of Adaptive NRMOvNext + StrongEngine $\Omega$
Full maximises strategy space within governance constraints, and
exceeds the RiskAdj baseline across short, medium, and long
horizons.

It says: ``do not close the future.''}
