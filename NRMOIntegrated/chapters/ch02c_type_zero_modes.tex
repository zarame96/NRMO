\section{Type ZERO Mode Definitions}
\label{sec:type-zero-modes-c2}

The Type ZERO governance layer assigns an operational mode based on
observed state. Modes are not constraints; they are gears for
preserving future degrees of freedom. CORE / VENTURE expand degrees
of freedom; MISSION / SHUTDOWN avoid irreversible boundaries.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{lXXX}
\toprule
\textbf{Mode} & \textbf{Purpose} & \textbf{Default policy} & \textbf{Transition trigger} \\
\midrule
CORE
& Normal operation (maximise degrees of freedom)
& Exploration / action permitted. Monitor only irreversible
boundary.
& $\mathrm{IRREV} = I_1$ and $\mathrm{VOL} \ge V_1$ $\to$ MISSION \\

VENTURE
& Exploration / expansion (new info / new actions)
& Test small, expand only within reversible range.
& $\mathrm{LOAD} = L_2$ or $\mathrm{VOL} = V_2$ $\to$ return to CORE \\

MISSION
& Control at boundary approach
& Organise action space; avoid irreversible-direction actions while
maintaining degrees of freedom.
& $\mathrm{IRREV} = I_0$ and $\mathrm{VOL} \le V_1$ $\to$ CORE \\

SHUTDOWN
& Stop (safe landing)
& Temporary halt, deferral, rest, third-party confirmation, etc.
& $\mathrm{IRREV} = I_1$ and $\mathrm{VOL} = V_2$ and
$\mathrm{LOAD} = L_2$ $\to$ immediate \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\caption[Type ZERO Mode Definitions]{Type ZERO Mode Definitions
(Table 2.1).}
\label{tab:type-zero-modes}
\end{table}

\nrmobox{Mode principle}{
Modes are not ``constraints'' --- they are \emph{gears} for
preserving future degrees of freedom. CORE / VENTURE expand degrees
of freedom; MISSION / SHUTDOWN avoid irreversible boundaries.
}
